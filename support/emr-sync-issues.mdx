EMR sync issues can delay claim submission and disrupt your entire billing workflow. When patient records, charges, or insurance information fail to transfer correctly between your electronic medical record system and Walker Health's billing platform, claims get stuck — or worse, submitted with errors. Most sync problems have straightforward causes and can be resolved quickly once identified.

## Common EMR Sync Issues

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Patient records not appearing in billing system">
    When a patient is registered in your EMR but their record does not show up in the billing system, it typically points to a sync configuration issue, a missing required field, or a filtering rule that is excluding the record.

    **Diagnosis steps:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Confirm the record is complete in your EMR">
        Check that the patient's record includes all required fields: full legal name, date of birth, gender, address, and at least one active insurance plan. Records with missing required fields are often excluded from syncing.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Check the sync log or error queue">
        Most EMR integrations generate a log of sync activity. Look for error entries or failed transfers associated with that patient's record.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Verify the sync schedule">
        Confirm when the last sync occurred. If the patient was registered after the last scheduled sync, they may simply not have been picked up yet.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Manually trigger a sync if available">
        Some integrations allow you to manually push a specific record or initiate an on-demand sync. Consult your EMR's documentation or contact your EMR vendor for guidance.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Report to Walker Health if unresolved">
        If the record still does not appear after the next sync cycle, submit a support ticket to Walker Health with the patient's name, date of birth, and EMR ID so we can investigate on our end.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Charges not transferring from EMR">
    Charge capture failures mean that services documented in your EMR are not making it into the billing system for claim submission. This is one of the most impactful sync issues because it directly delays revenue.

    **Diagnosis steps:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Confirm the charge was posted in the EMR">
        Verify that the service was fully documented and the charge was marked as complete or finalized in your EMR — not left in draft or unsigned status.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Check for CPT or HCPCS code mapping errors">
        Some EMR systems require procedure codes to be mapped to billing codes. If a code is unmapped or mapped incorrectly, it may be dropped during the transfer. Review your charge master or code mapping configuration.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review provider assignment">
        Make sure the rendering provider associated with the charge has an NPI and is correctly configured in your EMR. Charges linked to unconfigured providers often fail to transfer.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Check the date range of the sync">
        Confirm that the date of service falls within the active sync window. Very old charges may fall outside the default lookback period.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicate records created">
    Duplicate patient records occur when the same patient is created more than once in your EMR or billing system — sometimes due to minor differences in name spelling, date of birth entry, or a failed sync that resulted in a re-import.

    **Diagnosis steps:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Identify all duplicates">
        Search for the patient by multiple identifiers (name variations, date of birth, insurance ID) to find all duplicate records.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Determine the correct master record">
        Identify which record has the most complete and accurate information, and which has claims or billing history attached.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Do not delete records with billing history">
        Merging or deleting records with attached claims can cause downstream billing errors. Contact Walker Health before making any changes to records that have claim activity.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Merge or flag the duplicates">
        Follow your EMR's process for merging duplicate records, or flag them for your Walker Health billing specialist to handle the billing side of the merge.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Insurance information not syncing correctly">
    When insurance plan details, member IDs, or group numbers do not transfer accurately, claims are submitted with incorrect payer information — leading to rejections or misdirected payments.

    **Diagnosis steps:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Compare the insurance record in both systems">
        Open the patient's record in both your EMR and the billing system and compare the insurance fields side by side to identify exactly what is different.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Check for special characters or formatting issues">
        Some integration platforms strip or misinterpret certain characters (hyphens, spaces, letters vs. numbers) in member IDs or group numbers. Confirm the exact format required by the payer.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Verify the payer ID mapping">
        Payers are identified in billing systems by electronic payer IDs, not by name. Confirm that the payer in your EMR is mapped to the correct payer ID in the billing system.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Re-enter and re-sync if needed">
        If the data in your EMR is correct but not syncing properly, try re-saving the insurance record to trigger a fresh sync. If the issue persists, escalate to Walker Health.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Connection timeout errors">
    Timeout errors indicate that the integration between your EMR and Walker Health's billing platform was interrupted before the sync could complete. This can result in partial data transfers or no data transfer at all.

    **Diagnosis steps:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Check your internet connectivity">
        Confirm that your facility's internet connection is stable. A network outage or significant slowdown during a scheduled sync can cause timeout errors.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review the sync log for frequency and timing">
        Repeated timeouts at the same time of day may indicate a scheduled task conflict or a resource bottleneck on your server. Note the time and duration of the failures.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Restart the integration service if applicable">
        If your EMR uses a locally installed integration agent or connector, try restarting the service. Consult your EMR vendor's documentation for the correct process.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Notify Walker Health with the error details">
        Share the specific error messages, timestamps, and frequency of timeouts so our integration team can investigate from our end and coordinate with your EMR vendor if needed.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  Most sync issues are resolved within 1 business day once reported. The key is reporting them promptly — delayed reporting leads to claim submission backlogs that take significantly longer to clear.
</Note>

## How to Report an EMR Issue to Walker Health

When you encounter a sync issue that you cannot resolve with the steps above, submit a report to Walker Health using one of the following methods:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit a support ticket">
    Log in to your client portal and open a new support ticket. Select "EMR / Integration Issue" as the category and include as much detail as possible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Include the right details">
    Your report should include:
    - The name of your EMR system and version (if known)
    - The type of data affected (patients, charges, insurance, etc.)
    - The approximate date and time the issue started
    - Any error messages you have seen
    - The number of patients or claims affected
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach screenshots or log files">
    If your EMR provides sync logs or error reports, attach them to the ticket. Visual documentation speeds up diagnosis significantly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow up by phone for urgent issues">
    If claims are being delayed for a large number of patients or the issue has persisted for more than one business day, call your Walker Health account manager directly in addition to submitting a ticket.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For additional support or to connect with our technical integration team, visit our [contact page](/support/contact).

## Preventive Best Practices for Maintaining Clean EMR Data

Proactive data hygiene is the most effective way to reduce the frequency and impact of sync issues. Incorporate these practices into your daily operations:

- **Verify patient demographics at every visit.** Confirm that name, date of birth, address, and insurance information are current before each appointment.
- **Complete and sign notes promptly.** Unsigned or incomplete encounters cannot be billed and may not sync correctly. Set a policy for note completion within 24 hours of service.
- **Audit your charge master and code mappings quarterly.** Procedure codes change annually; outdated mappings cause charge transfer failures.
- **Train staff on correct data entry standards.** Consistent formatting — especially for insurance IDs and provider information — reduces sync errors significantly.
- **Review sync logs weekly.** Catching errors in the sync log before they affect billing is far easier than correcting them after the fact.
- **Communicate EMR updates or upgrades to Walker Health in advance.** System upgrades can temporarily disrupt integrations. Give our team at least two weeks' notice before major changes so we can prepare.

<Tip>
  Schedule a quarterly data quality review with your Walker Health billing specialist. A 30-minute call to review open sync errors and data quality trends can prevent the majority of recurring integration issues.
</Tip>
