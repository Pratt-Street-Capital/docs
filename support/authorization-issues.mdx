Authorization issues are among the most common — and most costly — challenges in behavioral health billing. Unlike a simple claim error, authorization problems often sit at the intersection of clinical care and payer requirements, meaning they require coordination between your clinical team and Walker Health's utilization review specialists. Understanding what to do in each scenario helps you respond quickly and protect both your patients and your revenue.

## Common Authorization Problems

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Authorization expired before services ended">
    This occurs when a patient continues receiving treatment after the authorized period has lapsed and a concurrent review was not completed in time to renew coverage.

    **What to do:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Stop and assess the gap">
        Identify the exact date the authorization expired and the date(s) of service that fall outside the authorized period.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Contact Walker Health immediately">
        Notify our UR team as soon as the gap is identified. Do not wait until billing to discover the issue. The sooner we know, the more options we have.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Request a retroactive authorization">
        Walker Health will contact the payer to request retroactive authorization for the lapsed dates. Success depends on the payer's policies and the strength of the clinical documentation.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Submit supporting documentation">
        Provide your clinical team's notes from the lapsed period to support the retroactive request. Strong documentation of medical necessity is critical.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Prepare for an appeal if needed">
        If the retroactive request is denied, Walker Health will evaluate appeal options and advise you on the likelihood of success.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Warning>
      Do not continue services on an expired authorization without contacting our UR team first. Every additional day of unauthorized service increases your financial exposure.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Payer denied initial authorization">
    An initial authorization denial means the payer reviewed the request and determined the proposed services do not meet their medical necessity criteria at the requested level of care.

    **What to do:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Review the denial letter">
        The denial notice will cite the specific clinical criteria that were not met. Share this with Walker Health and your clinical team.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Assess whether an appeal is appropriate">
        Walker Health will evaluate the denial reason and advise whether a formal appeal, peer-to-peer review, or an alternative level of care request is the best path forward.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Strengthen the clinical documentation">
        Work with your clinical staff to address any documentation gaps. Payers often deny requests when the clinical notes do not clearly demonstrate the severity, functional impairment, or risk factors that justify the requested level of care.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Request a peer-to-peer review if indicated">
        A peer-to-peer call between a payer's medical director and your treating clinician can be highly effective in reversing an initial denial. Walker Health will coordinate this on your behalf.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Concurrent review not completed in time">
    If Walker Health or your clinical team does not submit updated documentation before an active authorization expires, services may lapse into an unauthorized status.

    **What to do:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Notify Walker Health immediately">
        As soon as you realize a concurrent review was missed, contact our UR team. Time is the most important factor.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Submit updated clinical notes">
        Provide the most recent progress notes, updated treatment plan, and any other documentation required by the payer. Your UR coordinator will tell you exactly what is needed.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Request an emergency or expedited review">
        Many payers offer expedited concurrent review when treatment is ongoing. Walker Health will submit the request on an urgent basis.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Note>
      Walker Health tracks authorization expiration dates and initiates concurrent review requests proactively. If you are receiving notifications from us requesting updated clinical documentation, please respond promptly — delays on your end can result in coverage gaps.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Incorrect authorization number on claim">
    If the authorization number submitted on a claim does not match the payer's records, the claim will typically be denied or rejected — even if a valid authorization exists.

    **What to do:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Verify the correct authorization number">
        Log in to the payer portal or contact the payer directly to confirm the authorization number associated with the patient's treatment episode and dates of service.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Correct and resubmit the claim">
        Update the authorization number in your billing system and resubmit the claim. In most cases, this is a straightforward correction that resolves quickly.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Notify Walker Health">
        Let your billing specialist know so the correction can be tracked and confirmed in your claims reporting.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Services provided outside authorized dates">
    Payers only reimburse for services delivered within the specific date range listed on the authorization. Services provided before the start date or after the end date are not covered under that authorization.

    **What to do:**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Identify the unauthorized dates of service">
        Review the authorization letter to confirm the exact approved date range and flag any claims that fall outside those dates.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Determine whether a separate authorization exists">
        Check whether another authorization covers the out-of-range dates, or whether a new authorization request should be submitted retroactively.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Contact Walker Health for next steps">
        Depending on the circumstances, Walker Health may be able to request an amendment to the existing authorization or submit a retroactive request for the additional dates.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Urgent Situations: Treatment Has Started and Authorization Is Pending

If a patient has already begun receiving treatment and you do not yet have an authorization in place — whether because the request is still processing or because an emergency admission occurred — contact Walker Health immediately.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Call our UR hotline" icon="phone">
    For urgent authorization situations, call Walker Health's utilization review line directly. Do not wait to submit through the portal — call us first.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Gather clinical documentation now" icon="file-medical">
    While you wait for our team to respond, have your clinical staff compile the intake assessment, diagnosis, and initial treatment plan. We will need this immediately.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Many payers have provisions for emergency and urgent admissions that allow retroactive authorization requests within a defined window — typically 24 to 72 hours. Acting quickly is essential to staying within that window.

<Warning>
  Do not continue services on an expired authorization without contacting our UR team first. Even a single additional day of unauthorized service can complicate a retroactive request or result in a permanent denial for that date.
</Warning>

## How to Escalate Urgent Authorization Issues

If an authorization issue is threatening active patient care or involves a large number of affected claims, escalate through the following steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact your UR coordinator directly">
    Reach out by phone or direct message — not just through the portal ticket system — to make sure the urgency is understood.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request escalation to a UR supervisor">
    If your coordinator is unavailable or the situation requires more authority, ask to speak with the UR supervisor on duty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provide complete context">
    Include the patient name, payer, level of care, dates of service affected, and a brief description of the issue so the team can act immediately without having to ask follow-up questions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow up in writing">
    After your call, send an email summary of the conversation and the agreed next steps to create a documented record.
  </Step>
</Steps>
