Utilization review is a critical — and often time-sensitive — part of delivering behavioral health and substance use treatment services. Insurance payers require ongoing clinical justification to authorize and continue covering care, and the rules vary significantly from one payer to the next. The questions below cover the most common areas where providers need clarity when working with Walker Health's utilization review team.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is utilization review and why does it matter?">
    Utilization review (UR) is the process by which insurance payers evaluate whether the medical services being requested or provided are medically necessary and appropriate for the patient's clinical condition. In behavioral health and substance use treatment, this typically includes:

    - **Prior authorization:** Approval obtained before services begin
    - **Concurrent review:** Ongoing authorization as treatment continues
    - **Retrospective review:** Review of services that have already been provided

    Without proper utilization review, claims are frequently denied for lack of medical necessity — even when the care provided was clinically appropriate. Walker Health's UR team manages this process proactively to protect your revenue and minimize disruptions to patient care.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When should we contact Walker Health for a prior authorization?">
    You should contact Walker Health's UR team **before a patient begins treatment** whenever possible. Most payers require prior authorization for:

    - Residential treatment (RTC)
    - Partial hospitalization programs (PHP)
    - Intensive outpatient programs (IOP)
    - Detox and medically managed withdrawal
    - Higher-acuity outpatient services

    <Tip>
      Contact our UR team as soon as a patient is admitted or identified for a higher level of care — even if it is after hours. Early notification gives us the best chance of securing authorization before services begin.
    </Tip>

    For outpatient therapy and standard office visits, authorization requirements vary by payer. Your Walker Health account manager can provide a payer-specific guide for the insurers in your network.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How quickly can you obtain a prior authorization?">
    Turnaround times depend on the payer and the urgency of the request. In general:

    - **Standard requests:** 1 to 3 business days
    - **Urgent or expedited requests:** Same day to 24 hours, depending on payer policy
    - **Emergency admissions:** Walker Health can request concurrent authorization retroactively in cases where a patient was admitted emergently

    Walker Health submits authorization requests promptly and follows up with payers to avoid delays. If a payer is taking longer than expected, our team will notify you and advise on how to proceed while the authorization is pending.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if a prior authorization is denied?">
    A denial of prior authorization does not necessarily mean the end of the road. Walker Health's UR team will:

    1. Review the denial reason and assess the clinical basis for the request
    2. Determine whether an appeal or peer-to-peer review is appropriate
    3. Work with your clinical team to strengthen the supporting documentation
    4. Submit a formal appeal or arrange a peer-to-peer review with the payer's medical director

    <Warning>
      Do not continue or begin services following a prior authorization denial without first consulting our UR team. Providing services without authorization significantly increases the risk of non-payment.
    </Warning>

    In some cases, we may recommend requesting authorization at a different level of care or exploring an alternative payer pathway.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is a concurrent review and how often is it required?">
    A concurrent review is an ongoing authorization check that payers require while a patient is actively receiving treatment. The payer's clinical team periodically reviews the patient's progress and current condition to determine whether continued services remain medically necessary.

    Frequency varies by payer and level of care:

    - **Residential treatment:** Typically every 3 to 7 days
    - **PHP/IOP:** Every 5 to 14 days, depending on the payer
    - **Outpatient:** Less frequent, often every 30 to 60 days or by episode

    Walker Health tracks all active authorizations and initiates concurrent review requests before existing authorizations expire. Your clinical team will be contacted when updated documentation is needed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What documentation does the payer need for medical necessity?">
    Payers use established clinical criteria — most commonly ASAM (American Society of Addiction Medicine) or InterQual — to assess medical necessity. Documentation that supports these determinations typically includes:

    - **Biopsychosocial assessment**
    - **Current diagnosis (DSM-5 codes)**
    - **Treatment plan with measurable goals**
    - **Progress notes showing response to treatment**
    - **Physician or clinician attestation**
    - **Risk assessment and safety planning documentation**
    - **Functional impairment details**

    Walker Health will provide your clinical team with payer-specific documentation guidance to ensure the right information is captured and submitted in the format each payer prefers.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can Walker Health handle peer-to-peer review requests?">
    Walker Health can facilitate and coordinate peer-to-peer (P2P) reviews, but the actual conversation must take place between the payer's medical director and a licensed clinician or physician from your organization. Our UR team will:

    - Schedule the peer-to-peer call on your behalf
    - Prepare a clinical summary and talking points for your provider
    - Advise on the key criteria the payer is applying
    - Follow up after the call to track the outcome

    <Tip>
      Peer-to-peer reviews are one of the most effective tools for overturning authorization denials. Do not skip this step — it significantly improves approval rates when your clinical team is well-prepared.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What levels of care do you support for utilization review?">
    Walker Health provides utilization review support across the full behavioral health continuum of care, including:

    - Medically managed detox and withdrawal stabilization
    - Residential treatment (RTC)
    - Partial hospitalization programs (PHP)
    - Intensive outpatient programs (IOP)
    - Standard outpatient therapy
    - Medication-assisted treatment (MAT)
    - Crisis stabilization and observation units

    If you offer a specialized program or level of care not listed here, contact your account manager to confirm coverage under your service agreement.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if a patient's insurance changes mid-treatment?">
    Insurance changes during active treatment are common and require immediate attention. If a patient's insurance changes, notify Walker Health's UR team right away. We will:

    1. Verify benefits and eligibility under the new insurance plan
    2. Determine whether a new prior authorization is required
    3. Request authorization from the new payer, including any retroactive coverage if applicable
    4. Advise your team on any gaps in coverage or financial exposure

    Do not assume that an existing authorization carries over to a new payer — it does not. Prompt notification allows us to minimize coverage gaps and protect your revenue.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do we send clinical documentation to your UR team?">
    Walker Health accepts clinical documentation through the following secure methods:

    - **Secure client portal:** Upload documents directly through your dashboard
    - **Encrypted email:** Use the secure email link provided by your UR coordinator
    - **Fax:** Send to our dedicated UR fax line (provided during onboarding)

    <Note>
      Do not send protected health information (PHI) via standard unsecured email. All clinical documentation must be transmitted through a HIPAA-compliant channel.
    </Note>

    When submitting documentation, include the patient's name, date of birth, insurance ID, and the authorization number (if one has been issued) to ensure it is matched to the correct case promptly.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
