Provider credentialing is the foundation of your ability to bill insurance payers and receive in-network reimbursement rates. The process can be lengthy and detail-intensive, but having a dedicated credentialing partner means you can focus on patient care while we manage the paperwork. Below are the most common questions providers ask about credentialing and enrollment with Walker Health Services.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long does the credentialing process take?">
    The credentialing timeline varies significantly by payer. In general, you should plan for:

    - **Commercial insurance payers:** 60 to 120 days
    - **Medicaid:** 45 to 90 days, depending on the state
    - **Medicare:** 30 to 60 days via PECOS for most provider types
    - **TRICARE and other government payers:** 60 to 120+ days

    These are estimates based on typical payer processing times. Delays can occur due to missing documentation, payer backlogs, or credentialing committee meeting schedules. Walker Health tracks every application and follows up proactively to minimize unnecessary delays.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What documents are needed for credentialing?">
    Each payer has its own requirements, but a standard credentialing packet typically includes:

    - Current resume or curriculum vitae (CV)
    - Copy of professional license(s) and any relevant certifications
    - DEA registration (if applicable)
    - Malpractice insurance certificate with coverage history
    - National Provider Identifier (NPI)
    - CAQH ProView profile (completed and attested)
    - Education and training verification (diplomas, transcripts)
    - Work history for the past 5 to 10 years
    - Government-issued photo ID
    - Signed authorization and attestation forms

    Walker Health will provide you with a comprehensive document checklist during onboarding and will flag any payer-specific requirements that go beyond the standard list.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we bill while credentialing is in progress?">
    In most cases, you cannot bill a payer as an in-network provider until credentialing is fully approved. However, there are some options available during the credentialing period:

    - **Retroactive billing:** Some payers allow claims to be submitted back to the application date once credentialing is approved. Walker Health will advise you on which payers offer this option.
    - **Billing under a credentialed provider:** If another provider in your practice is already credentialed with the payer, services may be billable under their NPI in certain circumstances.
    - **Out-of-network billing:** You may be able to bill out-of-network while credentialing is pending, though reimbursement rates are typically lower.

    <Note>
      Billing policies during credentialing vary by payer and provider type. Contact your Walker Health credentialing specialist before submitting any claims for a provider whose credentialing is still in progress.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is CAQH and how does it work?">
    CAQH ProView is a centralized credentialing database used by most commercial insurance payers in the United States. It allows providers to enter their professional and personal information once and authorize participating payers to access it, eliminating the need to submit duplicate paperwork to each payer individually.

    To use CAQH:

    1. Register at [proview.caqh.org](https://proview.caqh.org) and obtain a CAQH ID
    2. Complete your provider profile with all required information
    3. Upload supporting documents (license, malpractice certificate, etc.)
    4. Attest to the accuracy of your profile (required every 120 days)
    5. Authorize specific payers to access your data

    Walker Health can help you set up or update your CAQH profile and ensure it is complete and current before submitting credentialing applications.

    <Tip>
      Keep your CAQH profile up to date at all times, even when you are not actively credentialing. Many payers pull CAQH data for re-credentialing automatically, and an expired or incomplete profile can cause delays.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do you handle re-credentialing deadlines?">
    Most payers require re-credentialing every two to three years. Walker Health tracks all re-credentialing deadlines for every provider in your practice and initiates the renewal process well in advance — typically 90 to 120 days before the expiration date — to ensure continuous network participation.

    You will be notified when re-credentialing is upcoming and what updated documentation may be required. Missing a re-credentialing deadline can result in temporary termination from a payer's network, which disrupts billing and patient care.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What payers do you credential with?">
    Walker Health credentials providers with a broad range of payers, including:

    - Commercial insurers (Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, BCBS plans, and others)
    - Medicaid managed care organizations (MCOs) across multiple states
    - Medicare (via PECOS)
    - TRICARE and other federal programs
    - Behavioral health carve-out plans (Optum, Magellan, Beacon Health Options, etc.)
    - State-specific plans and local HMOs

    If you have a specific payer in mind, contact your credentialing specialist to confirm that payer is within our scope and to discuss the application process.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if a provider leaves our practice?">
    When a provider departs your organization, you should notify Walker Health immediately. Depending on your payer contracts and the circumstances of the departure, you may need to:

    - **Notify payers** of the provider's termination from your group
    - **Reassign or transfer patients** as required by payer contracts
    - **Remove the provider** from your group's roster with each payer
    - **Retain records** of the provider's credentialing file for the required period

    <Warning>
      Continuing to submit claims under a provider who has left your practice — even inadvertently — can be considered fraudulent billing. Always notify Walker Health of provider departures promptly so we can update payer rosters.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do you handle facility credentialing or only individual providers?">
    Walker Health handles both individual provider credentialing and facility-level credentialing (also called organizational credentialing or group enrollment). Facility credentialing typically involves:

    - Enrolling your organization as a billing entity with each payer
    - Obtaining or updating your group NPI (Type 2) enrollment
    - Submitting facility licensure and accreditation documents
    - Linking individual provider NPIs to your group contract

    If you are opening a new location or adding a new level of care, facility credentialing may be required before you can bill under that site. Contact your credentialing specialist early in the process to avoid delays.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between credentialing and enrollment?">
    These terms are often used interchangeably, but they refer to distinct processes:

    - **Credentialing** is the process of verifying a provider's qualifications, education, licensure, and professional history against payer standards. It is primarily a clinical verification process.
    - **Enrollment** (also called contracting or participating provider agreement) is the administrative process of formally joining a payer's network and establishing the contractual terms under which you will be reimbursed.

    In practice, both processes are typically required before a provider can bill in-network, and Walker Health manages both simultaneously on your behalf.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How will we know when a provider is approved?">
    Walker Health notifies you as soon as a credentialing or enrollment approval is received from a payer. You will receive:

    - **Confirmation of the effective date** (the date from which in-network billing is valid)
    - **Provider network ID or contract number** (if issued by the payer)
    - **Retroactive billing eligibility** (if applicable)
    - **Updated roster confirmation** for your records

    Approvals are also tracked in your credentialing dashboard, where you can view the status of every pending application at any time. If you need a status update before you hear from us, log in to your portal or contact your credentialing specialist directly.
  </Accordion>
