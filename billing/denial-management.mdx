Claim denials are one of the most significant threats to your practice's revenue cycle. Left unaddressed, a denied claim quickly becomes lost revenue — and the longer it sits, the harder it is to recover. Walker Health Services takes a proactive approach to denial management, identifying denied claims immediately, analyzing their root cause, and taking swift corrective action to recover every dollar your practice has earned.

<Warning>
  Ignoring denials results in permanent revenue loss. Every denial requires action. Once timely filing or appeal deadlines pass, the opportunity to recover that revenue is gone for good.
</Warning>

## Common Denial Reasons

Understanding why claims are denied is the first step toward preventing future denials. The most common reasons your claims may be rejected or denied include:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Missing or Invalid Information">
    Claims submitted with incomplete or inaccurate data — such as an incorrect date of birth, invalid NPI, missing modifier, or wrong insurance ID — are frequently rejected by payers before they even reach adjudication. Our coding review process catches most of these issues before submission, but when a payer returns a claim for missing information, we correct and resubmit it promptly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Eligibility Issues">
    Denials due to eligibility occur when a patient's coverage is inactive, the service is not covered under their plan, or the claim is submitted under the wrong payer or policy number. These denials are largely preventable through thorough eligibility verification prior to the date of service.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical Necessity Not Established">
    Payers may deny a claim if the submitted documentation does not sufficiently support the medical necessity of the service provided. This is especially common in behavioral health, where payers scrutinize level-of-care decisions for residential, PHP, and IOP services. Appeals for these denials require detailed clinical documentation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Timely Filing Exceeded">
    If a claim is submitted after the payer's timely filing deadline, it will typically be denied as non-payable. These denials are difficult to overturn and represent direct revenue loss. Walker Health monitors filing windows closely to prevent this outcome.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Duplicate Claim">
    A duplicate denial occurs when a payer believes a claim has already been submitted and adjudicated for the same patient, date of service, and procedure. This can happen due to resubmission errors or system issues. Our team identifies duplicate denials and works with the payer to resolve billing conflicts and confirm correct adjudication.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Our Denial Management Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Denial Identified">
    Denied claims are flagged automatically through our clearinghouse and payer portals. Our team reviews every denial as it comes in — no claim sits unworked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Root Cause Analysis">
    We categorize each denial by reason code and analyze the underlying cause. This step determines whether the appropriate resolution is a corrected claim, a formal appeal, or additional documentation from your clinical team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Corrected Claim or Appeal Filed">
    Depending on the denial type, we either resubmit a corrected claim or prepare a formal written appeal with supporting documentation. Appeals for medical necessity denials are submitted with clinical records and level-of-care criteria where applicable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow-Up with Payer">
    After submission, we track the status of every corrected claim and appeal. Our team contacts payers directly when responses are delayed or when additional information is requested during the review process.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Revenue Recovered">
    Once the payer adjudicates the corrected or appealed claim, payment is posted and reconciled. The outcome is documented in your reporting so you have full visibility into recovered revenue.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Denial Prevention Tips

Recovering denied claims is important, but preventing them in the first place is even better for your practice's cash flow. Here are key steps you can take to reduce your denial rate:

- **Verify eligibility before every date of service**, not just at intake or re-enrollment periods
- **Obtain and document prior authorizations** for all services that require them, and track authorization expiration dates
- **Ensure clinical documentation supports level of care** for all residential, PHP, and IOP admissions
- **Review your superbills and encounter forms** regularly to confirm service codes and modifiers are current and accurate
- **Communicate with our billing team quickly** when payers request medical records or additional information — delays cost you appeal windows
