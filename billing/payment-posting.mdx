Accurate payment posting is the foundation of a healthy revenue cycle. When payments are posted correctly and promptly, your financial records reflect your true revenue, your accounts receivable stays clean, and your team can make informed decisions about collections and cash flow. Walker Health Services handles all aspects of payment posting — from insurance remittances to patient balances — so your books are always up to date and reconciled.

## Types of Payments Posted

Walker Health posts all payment types that flow into your practice:

- **Insurance ERA/EFT Payments** — Electronic remittance advices (ERAs) paired with electronic funds transfers (EFTs) are the fastest and most accurate payment type. We retrieve and post these automatically.
- **Manual EOB Payments** — Paper or portal-based explanations of benefits from payers who do not issue ERAs are manually reviewed, keyed, and reconciled against your claims.
- **Patient Payments** — Co-pays, deductibles, co-insurance, and self-pay balances collected at the time of service or in response to patient statements are posted to the correct account and visit.
- **Credit Card Payments** — Payments made by patients via phone, mail, or online portal are posted accurately and matched to the corresponding patient balance.

## Payment Posting Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive ERA or EOB">
    Electronic remittances are retrieved automatically from your clearinghouse or payer portals on a daily basis. Paper EOBs received by mail or through payer websites are logged and queued for manual posting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match to Claims">
    Each payment line is matched to the corresponding submitted claim using the claim number, patient ID, date of service, and procedure code. Unmatched payments are flagged for review before posting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post Payments">
    Approved payments are posted to the correct patient account and claim in your practice management system. Contractual adjustments are applied according to your payer fee schedules.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Adjustments and Write-Offs">
    Contractual write-offs, non-covered service adjustments, and other balance modifications are applied in accordance with your payer contracts and internal policies. No write-offs are applied without documented justification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flag Underpayments for Follow-Up">
    When a payer reimburses less than the contracted rate, the variance is flagged and escalated. Our team contacts the payer to dispute underpayments and recover the correct contracted amount on your behalf.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## ERA vs. Manual EOB

Understanding the difference between these two remittance types helps you appreciate why electronic setup matters for your cash flow.

| | **ERA (Electronic Remittance Advice)** | **Manual EOB** |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery** | Automatically delivered via clearinghouse | Paper mail or manual payer portal download |
| **Posting Speed** | Same day or next day | 1–3 business days after receipt |
| **Error Risk** | Low — automated matching | Higher — manual keying required |
| **Recommended** | Yes — for all eligible payers | Only when ERA is unavailable |

<Tip>
  Setting up electronic remittance (ERA) and electronic funds transfer (EFT) with every payer that supports it will significantly speed up your payment posting cycle, reduce manual errors, and give you faster access to your revenue. Ask your Walker Health contact to help you enroll in ERA/EFT with any payers where it is not yet active.
</Tip>

## Patient Balance Statements

After insurance payments are posted and all applicable adjustments are applied, any remaining patient responsibility — co-pays, deductibles, co-insurance, or self-pay balances — is billed directly to the patient.

Walker Health generates patient statements on a regular billing cycle and ensures they are clear, accurate, and compliant with your practice's billing policies. Statements reflect only the balance remaining after insurance has paid its portion, and they include enough detail for patients to understand what they owe and why.

If a patient balance goes unpaid after the initial statement cycle, follow-up statements are generated and the account is escalated according to your practice's collections protocol. Our goal is to recover patient balances respectfully and efficiently while protecting the patient relationships your clinical team has worked to build.
