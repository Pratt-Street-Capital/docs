Provider enrollment is the process of formally applying to become an in-network provider with insurance payers. Until this process is complete and an effective date is established, your providers cannot bill payers at contracted in-network rates — and in many cases cannot bill them at all. Walker Health Services manages every step of enrollment on your behalf, from gathering initial documentation to filing approval letters and configuring your billing setup once a contract is in place.

## What You Need to Get Started

Before enrollment applications can be submitted, you will need to have the following documentation ready for each provider. Our team will guide you through gathering everything and flag any items that need to be obtained before applications can move forward.

- **NPI numbers** — both the individual provider's Type 1 NPI and your organization's Type 2 (group) NPI
- **CAQH profile** — an active Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare profile, which most commercial payers require as part of the application process
- **State licenses** — current, active licensure in each state where the provider will practice
- **DEA certificate** — if the provider is authorized to prescribe controlled substances
- **Malpractice insurance** — a current certificate of professional liability coverage with your organization listed
- **W-9** — a completed IRS W-9 for your practice or organization

## Our Enrollment Process

Once documentation is in hand, our team follows a structured, six-step process to move each application from submission to approval as efficiently as possible.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collect Provider Documentation">
    We work with you to gather all required credentialing documents for each provider, verify that licenses and certificates are current, and organize the file before any applications are submitted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create or Update CAQH Profile">
    We set up a new CAQH profile or update an existing one with current information. Because most commercial payers pull directly from CAQH, keeping this profile accurate and attested is critical to avoiding processing delays.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Target Payers">
    We work with you to identify which payers are most important to your patient population and business goals — prioritizing those with the highest volume or most favorable reimbursement rates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Payer Applications">
    We complete and submit enrollment applications to each target payer, whether through CAQH, the payer's online portal, or paper application — following each payer's specific requirements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track Status and Respond to Payer Requests">
    We actively monitor the status of every pending application and respond promptly to any additional information requests from payers. Delays most commonly occur when payer requests go unanswered — our team eliminates that risk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive and File Approval Letters and Effective Dates">
    When an application is approved, we collect the approval letter, record the effective date, and communicate both to your team. Billing for that payer can begin on the effective date — not the approval date.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Enrollment Timelines

Payer enrollment timelines vary significantly and are ultimately controlled by each payer's internal processing times. As a general guide:

- **Commercial payers** (Aetna, BCBS, Cigna, UHC, etc.) typically take **60–120 days** from application submission to approval
- **Medicaid** enrollment timelines vary by state and can range from **90–180 days or longer**
- **Medicare** (PECOS enrollment) typically takes **60–120 days**, but can extend further during periods of high volume

These timelines assume a complete application with no missing information. Incomplete applications or slow responses to payer requests can add weeks or months to the process.

<Tip>
  Ensure your CAQH profile is updated and re-attested every 120 days. Many payers will automatically pause or reject applications tied to an expired CAQH profile, adding unnecessary delays to your enrollment timeline.
</Tip>

## What Happens After Approval

Approval is not the finish line — it is the starting point for billing with that payer. Once you receive an approval, Walker Health Services will:

1. **Record the effective date** — the date from which in-network billing is permitted; this is separate from, and often later than, the approval date
2. **Configure your billing setup** — ensuring the contracted payer rates, provider identifiers, and taxonomy codes are correctly reflected in your practice management or billing system
3. **Support your first claim submission** — verifying that the first claim submitted to a newly enrolled payer is clean and complete to establish a smooth billing relationship from the start

<Info>
  Keep in mind that services rendered before your provider's effective date cannot be retroactively billed at in-network rates, even if the application was in process at the time. Scheduling new patients only after the effective date is confirmed helps protect your revenue.
</Info>
