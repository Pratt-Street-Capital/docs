Medical necessity is the foundation of insurance reimbursement for behavioral health services. Before a payer will authorize or pay for treatment — whether that's a residential stay, a detox admission, or an intensive outpatient program — they require evidence that the level of care is clinically justified for that specific patient at that specific point in time. Without a strong medical necessity determination, even the most thorough clinical care can go uncompensated. Walker Health Services manages this process end to end, ensuring your cases are presented to payers in the strongest possible light.

## What Medical Necessity Means in Behavioral Health

In behavioral health and substance use treatment, medical necessity is not simply a diagnosis. Payers evaluate whether the patient's clinical presentation — their severity of illness, level of functioning, risk factors, and treatment history — requires the intensity of care being requested. A patient may have a qualifying diagnosis but still be denied if the documentation doesn't clearly demonstrate why they cannot be safely treated at a lower level of care.

This distinction matters enormously. Your clinical team may know that a patient needs residential treatment, but the payer's reviewer is evaluating a written record. The quality, completeness, and framing of that documentation directly determines whether authorization is granted.

## Common Payer Criteria

Payers use a variety of clinical guidelines to evaluate medical necessity. Our team is experienced with all of the major frameworks in use today:

- **ASAM Criteria (American Society of Addiction Medicine)** — The most widely used framework for substance use disorder treatment; assesses patients across six dimensions including intoxication/withdrawal potential, biomedical conditions, emotional/behavioral conditions, readiness to change, relapse potential, and recovery environment.
- **Milliman Care Guidelines** — Evidence-based guidelines used by many commercial and managed care payers to determine appropriate levels of care.
- **InterQual** — Clinical decision support criteria used by a broad range of health plans and utilization management organizations.
- **Payer-Specific Guidelines** — Many large payers, including Cigna, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Magellan, maintain proprietary medical necessity criteria that supplement or replace industry-standard tools. Our team stays current on these requirements.

## Our Medical Necessity Review Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review Clinical Documentation">
    We begin by reviewing all available clinical documentation, including the patient's biopsychosocial assessment, intake evaluation, psychiatric evaluation (if applicable), prior treatment history, and any relevant medical records. We identify gaps or areas that need to be strengthened before submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess Against Payer Criteria">
    We map the patient's clinical presentation against the specific criteria used by their insurance payer. This includes determining the appropriate ASAM level of care, reviewing dimensional scoring, and confirming that the documentation supports the requested level of treatment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit to Payer">
    We submit the medical necessity request to the payer through the required channel — whether that's a web portal, fax, or phone-based review — along with all supporting clinical documentation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Respond to Requests for Information">
    Payers frequently request additional information before rendering a decision. Our team responds promptly to these requests with targeted clinical details that address the payer's specific concerns.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure Approval">
    Once medical necessity is confirmed, we document the authorization, including the approved level of care, authorized dates of service, and any conditions attached to the approval. This information is handed off directly to your billing workflow.
  </Step>
</Steps>

|---|---|---|

</Tip>

## When Medical Necessity Is Denied

If a payer denies medical necessity, you have the right to appeal. Walker Health Services manages the full appeals process on your behalf, including:

- **Level 1 Appeals** — A formal written appeal submitted to the payer's internal review team, typically with additional clinical documentation and a written argument addressing the specific basis for denial.
- **Peer-to-Peer Reviews** — A direct conversation between our clinical staff (or your treating clinician) and the payer's medical director to discuss the clinical rationale for the requested level of care.
- **Level 2 and External Appeals** — If internal appeals are exhausted, we escalate to external independent review organizations (IROs) or state insurance regulators as appropriate.

Denials are not the end of the road. Many denied authorizations are overturned on appeal when the clinical case is properly presented and documented.
