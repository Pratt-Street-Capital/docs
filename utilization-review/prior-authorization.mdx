Prior authorization — commonly referred to as prior auth or PA — is a requirement imposed by most commercial insurers and managed behavioral health organizations (MBHOs) before treatment can begin and be reimbursed. The payer must review and approve the clinical justification for a requested level of care before they are obligated to pay for it. Managing this process efficiently is essential to protecting your revenue and ensuring your patients receive uninterrupted care. Walker Health Services handles the entire prior authorization workflow from the moment a patient is admitted through every concurrent review until discharge.

## When Prior Authorization Is Required

Prior authorization requirements vary by payer and plan, but in behavioral health settings, authorization is typically required for:

- Any level of residential or inpatient care (ASAM 3.5, 3.7)
- Partial hospitalization programs (PHP / ASAM 2.5)
- Intensive outpatient programs (IOP / ASAM 2.1)
- Medication-assisted treatment (MAT) in some plans
- Extended outpatient episodes beyond a threshold number of visits
- Step-downs between levels of care

Some payers also require authorization for standard outpatient services beyond an initial number of covered visits. Our team verifies each patient's specific authorization requirements at intake, before any services are rendered.

## Our Authorization Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify Payer Requirements at Intake">
    We verify the patient's insurance coverage and confirm the specific prior authorization requirements for their plan and the requested level of care. This includes identifying the correct payer portal, fax number, or phone line for submission, and noting any plan-specific deadlines or documentation requirements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Gather Clinical Documentation">
    We work with your clinical team to compile all required documentation, including the biopsychosocial assessment, intake evaluation, ASAM dimensional scoring, and any other materials the payer requires. We review these documents for completeness and flag any gaps before submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the PA Request">
    We submit the prior authorization request to the payer through the appropriate channel, within the payer's required timeframe. For emergency or urgent admissions, we follow expedited submission procedures to minimize any risk of a gap in authorization coverage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow Up With Payer">
    We actively monitor the status of every open PA request and follow up with the payer according to their review timelines. We do not wait for the payer to reach out to us — we proactively track each case to prevent delays.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive Auth Number and Document">
    Once the payer approves the request, we record the authorization number, approved level of care, authorized units or days, and any conditions or limitations. This information is entered into your billing system and shared with the appropriate members of your team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Manage Concurrent Reviews">
    Most authorizations cover a limited number of days or treatment units. Our team manages the ongoing concurrent review process to ensure continued authorization throughout the patient's episode of care. See [Ongoing Case Review](/utilization-review/case-review) for details.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Authorization Types

Not all authorizations are the same. Our team manages all three authorization types that arise in behavioral health treatment:

- **Initial Authorization** — The first approval obtained before or at the time of admission. This covers the initial period of treatment and must be secured before claims can be submitted for those dates of service.
- **Concurrent Review** — An ongoing authorization review that occurs while the patient is still in treatment. Payers require updated clinical information to justify continued stay beyond the initially authorized period. Concurrent reviews must be submitted before the current authorization expires.
- **Retrospective Review** — In cases where treatment has already begun without a prior authorization (such as an emergency admission), a retrospective review is submitted after the fact. These require strong clinical documentation and are subject to stricter scrutiny.

<Warning>
  Starting treatment without a confirmed prior authorization — even when you believe coverage is in place — creates significant financial risk. If a payer denies a retrospective authorization request, all claims for those dates of service may be denied and may not be recoverable on appeal. Always confirm authorization before the patient's start date whenever possible, and contact Walker Health Services immediately for emergency admissions so we can initiate an expedited review.
</Warning>

## Concurrent Review Management

Most payers authorize behavioral health services in short increments — often just a few days at a time for residential care. Our team manages the full concurrent review cycle for every active patient, including:

- Tracking every authorization's expiration date and submitting renewals before the current authorization lapses
- Preparing updated clinical summaries that reflect the patient's current presentation, progress in treatment, and continued medical necessity
- Responding promptly to payer requests for additional information during the review process
- Escalating to peer-to-peer reviews when a payer proposes a step-down or denial of continued stay
