Securing the initial authorization is only the beginning. Once a patient is admitted to treatment, maintaining continuous payer coverage requires active, ongoing clinical case management throughout their entire episode of care. Most payers authorize behavioral health services in short increments and require regular clinical updates to justify continued stay. Without consistent and timely concurrent reviews, your organization risks retroactive denials that can result in significant uncompensated care. Walker Health Services manages the full ongoing case review process so that coverage stays in place from admission through discharge.

## What Concurrent Review Means and Why It Matters

Concurrent review is the process by which a payer evaluates whether continued treatment at the current level of care remains medically necessary, based on the patient's evolving clinical picture. Unlike the initial authorization — which is submitted once at or before admission — concurrent reviews happen repeatedly throughout the episode of care, often every two to five days for residential levels of care and every one to two weeks for lower levels.

Each concurrent review is an opportunity for a payer to approve continued stay, recommend a step-down to a lower level of care, or deny continued coverage. Our team's job is to ensure that every review is submitted on time, supported by strong clinical documentation, and followed up aggressively until a decision is rendered.

## Our Case Review Process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Track Authorization Expiration Dates">
    We maintain a real-time tracking log of every active authorization across your census. As each authorization approaches its expiration date, we initiate the concurrent review process with enough lead time to prevent any gap in coverage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare Clinical Updates">
    We work with your clinical staff to gather updated patient information, including progress notes, group therapy participation, medication updates, any significant behavioral or medical changes, and updated ASAM dimensional scoring. We synthesize this information into a clinical update that clearly demonstrates continued medical necessity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Concurrent Review Requests">
    We submit the concurrent review request to the payer before the current authorization expires, through the appropriate channel and within any payer-specified deadlines. We document the submission date, method, and the name of the payer representative when applicable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Respond to Peer-to-Peer Review Requests">
    When a payer's clinical reviewer proposes a step-down or denial of continued stay, we facilitate a peer-to-peer review between the payer's medical director and the appropriate clinical staff at your facility. We coordinate scheduling, prepare the clinical team with the relevant case details, and document the outcome.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document Outcomes">
    Every concurrent review decision — whether an approval, a step-down recommendation, or a denial — is documented in detail, including the authorization number, approved dates, the name of the reviewing clinician, and any conditions attached to the approval. This record becomes part of the patient's billing file.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Peer-to-Peer Reviews

When a payer's utilization reviewer disagrees with the treating team's clinical judgment, they may offer — or your team may request — a peer-to-peer review. This is a scheduled phone call between the payer's medical director and a physician, nurse, or licensed clinician at your facility. Peer-to-peer reviews are one of the most effective tools available for overturning proposed step-downs and denials.

Walker Health Services facilitates peer-to-peer reviews by:

- Identifying when a peer-to-peer is warranted and requesting it promptly from the payer
- Coordinating availability between your clinical staff and the payer's medical director
- Preparing a concise clinical briefing for your clinician that highlights the key points to address during the call
- Documenting the outcome and updating the authorization record accordingly

We strongly encourage providers to participate in peer-to-peer reviews whenever they are offered. Approvals following peer-to-peer calls are common, and the clinical conversation creates a documented record that supports the case if further appeals become necessary.

## Discharge Planning Documentation

Payers pay close attention to discharge planning as part of the concurrent review process. Evidence that your clinical team is actively working toward an appropriate level of step-down or discharge — and that a realistic discharge plan is in place — strengthens the medical necessity case for continued stay at the current level. Our team ensures that discharge planning updates are included in every concurrent review submission and are framed in a way that satisfies payer expectations without undermining the clinical justification for ongoing care.

<Note>
  Timely concurrent reviews are your most important protection against retroactive denials. If a current authorization lapses before a renewal is approved, the payer may deny claims for the days of service that fall outside the authorized period — even if the patient was in active treatment and would otherwise have qualified for continued stay. Our team's authorization tracking process is specifically designed to prevent this scenario. We submit renewals proactively, follow up aggressively, and alert your team immediately if a payer delays a decision beyond the authorization expiration date so that escalation steps can be taken.
</Note>

## How Case Review Data Feeds Into Billing

Every outcome from the concurrent review process is directly connected to your billing workflow. When our UR team secures a renewed authorization, that authorization number, the approved level of care, and the covered dates of service are immediately available to your billing team — or to Walker Health's billing team if we are also managing your revenue cycle. This ensures that claims are submitted only for authorized dates of service, with the correct authorization references, reducing the risk of denials at the claim level and creating a clean audit trail for every billed episode of care.
