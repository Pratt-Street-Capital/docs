Beyond tracking individual claims, Walker Health helps you understand your revenue performance over time. Analytics transform raw billing data into meaningful insight — showing you where your revenue is coming from, where it is leaking out, and how your financial health compares to industry benchmarks. For behavioral health and residential treatment providers, this level of visibility is essential for sustainable growth and confident payer negotiations.

## Key Analytics Areas

Walker Health tracks five core analytics areas that together give you a complete picture of your financial performance.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Net Collection Rate" icon="percent">
    The percentage of collectible revenue that your practice actually collects after adjustments. A net collection rate of **95% or higher** is the industry benchmark for a well-managed behavioral health practice. Rates below this threshold signal billing inefficiencies, underpayments, or write-offs that deserve investigation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Days in AR" icon="hourglass-start">
    The average number of days between claim submission and payment receipt. For behavioral health providers, the target is **under 30 days**. A rising days-in-AR figure often points to slow payer adjudication, missing authorizations, or a backlog of unpursued claims.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Denial Rate" icon="circle-exclamation">
    The percentage of submitted claims that are denied by a payer. The industry benchmark is **under 5%**. Tracking your denial rate over time — and by payer — helps isolate the root causes of revenue leakage so they can be corrected at the source.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Payer Mix Analysis" icon="chart-pie">
    A breakdown of your total revenue by insurance carrier. Understanding your payer mix helps you see which payers are contributing most to your bottom line and where reimbursement rates may be pulling your average down. This data is especially valuable before contract renewal conversations.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Level of Care Revenue Breakdown" icon="stairs">
    A segmentation of your revenue by level of care — detox, residential, partial hospitalization (PHP), intensive outpatient (IOP), and outpatient (OP). This view shows which programs are driving the most revenue and where billing gaps or reimbursement shortfalls may exist across your continuum of care.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Monthly Performance Review

Each month, your Walker Health account manager schedules a performance review call to walk through your analytics together. These calls are structured, consistent, and designed to give you actionable insight — not just numbers.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pre-Call Report Package">
    Before each call, your account manager prepares a summary report covering your key metrics for the prior month, including net collection rate, denial rate, days in AR, and any notable payer activity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Metrics Review">
    During the call, your account manager reviews each metric with you, explains any movement up or down, and provides context based on payer behavior, claim volume, or operational changes at your practice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Issue Identification">
    If any metric is trending in the wrong direction, your account manager will identify the likely cause and outline the steps being taken to correct it — whether that is a denial appeal, a credentialing follow-up, or an authorization process adjustment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Action Items and Next Steps">
    Every call ends with a clear list of action items, split between what Walker Health is handling and anything that requires input or action from your team. These items are documented and revisited at the following month's review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Year-Over-Year Trending

Walker Health tracks your practice's performance longitudinally so you can see how far you have come and where growth opportunities remain. Year-over-year trending reports compare your current metrics to the same period in prior years, accounting for changes in claim volume, payer mix, and level-of-care utilization.

This historical view is particularly valuable when evaluating the impact of a new payer contract, a new clinical program, or a staffing or operational change. Your account manager can pull year-over-year comparisons on demand or include them in your quarterly business reviews.

<Note>
  Your analytics data is a powerful asset when negotiating with payers. Bring your payer performance report and level-of-care revenue breakdown to contract renewal discussions. Data on your reimbursement rates, denial patterns, and patient volume gives you a factual foundation for requesting rate increases and improved contract terms.
</Note>

## Quarterly Payer Rate Comparison

Reimbursement rates are not static, and the difference between what one payer offers and what another pays for the same service can be significant. Walker Health tracks contracted and effective reimbursement rates across all of your payers so you always know where you stand.

<Tip>
  Ask your account manager for a quarterly payer rate comparison to ensure you're getting fair reimbursements. If a payer is consistently reimbursing below market rates for your region and level of care, Walker Health can support you in preparing for a contract renegotiation.
</Tip>
